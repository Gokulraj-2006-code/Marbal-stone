01 · Buyer priority

Start with the strongest fit.

The order uses the three fictional buyers in the brief. Priority is based on project fit, timing and the information still needed.

Priority 1 · P01

Interior designer

Need: 2 custom basins for a premium home in Pune; home due in 60 days.

  • Why first: premium fit, custom quantity and enough lead time for a quote conversation.
  • Ask next: dimensions, budget, destination and finish requirements.
  • Reach similar: designer networks, architecture/interior communities and project-led referrals.
Priority 2 · P02

Boutique hotel buyer

Need: 12 bathroom units in 10 days; open to different materials.

  • Why second: high-volume opportunity, but the 10-day requirement needs feasibility checking.
  • Ask next: exact destination, unit specification, budget and required date.
  • Reach similar: hospitality procurement, hotel design teams and project contractors.
Priority 3 · P03

Homeowner

Need: 1 basin under ₹5,000 within 3 days; may only be exploring.

  • Why third: sample estimate and requested timing may not fit; confirm before investing sales effort.
  • Ask next: destination, dimensions, exact date and willingness to discuss a quote.
  • Reach similar: design-led social content, renovation communities and referrals.

Research notes

Design questions worth asking.

These are general research insights used to improve the enquiry prompts; the assignment pack remains the source of truth for EX—B01's fictional commercial details.

Natural variation

Natural stone can vary in colour and appearance, so buyers benefit from discussing samples and material expectations early.

Fit before price

Dimensions, destination and project timing are useful before a supplier can sensibly discuss a quote path.

Care + finish

Stone selection can involve finish, porosity, maintenance and installation questions; the right questions depend on the specific stone.
